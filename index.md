# Blackkeep Privacy Policy
Last updated: 2026-09-30

Blackkeep is a game for iPhone made by Christopher Anderson Smith, the developer named on the
App Store ("I", "me" below). This page explains what happens to your information when you play.

## The short version
There are no accounts, and I don't ask for your name, email or anything else about you. Your
progress is saved only on your iPhone. The game uses a few outside services, each for one job:
- Apple and RevenueCat handle purchases, so you get what you paid for and can restore it.
- Google AdMob shows an ad only when you tap "Watch ad" to get a reward. You choose whether ads
  can use your advertising ID; if you say no, you still get the reward.
- Sentry receives a report when the game crashes, so I can fix it, but only if you say yes.
- Apple's Game Center runs the leaderboards, if you're signed in to it.
I don't sell your information, and I don't use it for anything but running and fixing the game.

## What I can see
I can't see who you are. The services above use random IDs instead of your name or email, and
I don't link those IDs to you. What I see is limited to: crash reports (below), a list of
purchases per random ID (below), totals from Google about how many ads were shown, and anything
you email me.

## Purchases
Purchases are made through Apple. Apple takes the payment; I never see your card, your name or
your Apple Account. To check what you own, the game uses RevenueCat, a service that keeps track
of in-app purchases. RevenueCat receives:
- a random ID it creates for this copy of the game (a new one if you delete and reinstall);
- the purchases made with it: which item, when, the price, currency and country; and
- basic technical details such as the app and iOS version.
I use this to unlock what you bought, to restore it (Settings > Restore Purchases), to spot
refunds and fraud, and to see how many of each item sell. RevenueCat keeps these records as long
as they're needed to keep your purchases working and for accounting. RevenueCat's privacy
policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy
The game remembers what you own on your iPhone too, so your purchases work offline.

## Ads (only if you choose to watch one)
The game never shows an ad on its own: no banners, no pop-up ads, no ads at launch or during a
battle. An ad plays only after you tap "Watch ad" on one of the game's reward offers, for
example after a run ends or between the acts of a Siege. Every offer is optional, and saying no
costs you nothing. Ads come from Google AdMob. Each time you open the game, Google's ad software
starts only when you tap "Watch ad" (it then stays on until you close the game, to have the next
ad ready). If you own the Blood Pact, the game never starts it at all.

The first time you tap "Watch ad":
- If you're in the UK, the EEA or Switzerland, Google's consent form asks what Google may do
  with your information. Until you've answered it, the game doesn't ask Google for any ad.
- Your iPhone asks whether Blackkeep may track you ("Allow" or "Ask App Not to Track").
  If you allow it, Google may use your iPhone's advertising ID to choose ads for you.
  Otherwise, the game asks Google only for ads that aren't personalised. Either way you get
  the same reward.

Once you've tapped "Watch ad", Google's software (including its consent form) may collect your
IP address (which gives a rough location), device identifiers (the advertising ID only if you
allowed tracking; otherwise identifiers that are limited to this app or developer), the ads you
see and how you interact with them, and performance and crash information about the ad
software. Google uses this to show ads, measure them, prevent fraud and improve its services.
How Google uses this information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and
Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

To change your choices later:
- Tracking: iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking, then Blackkeep.
- Google's choices: if you're in the UK, the EEA, Switzerland or a US state with its own privacy
  law, the game's Settings has a "Privacy choices" option (it appears after your first "Watch
  ad" tap). It opens Google's form, where you can change your consent or opt out of
  personalised ads.
- Not watching ads: just don't tap "Watch ad". Nothing in the game needs an ad.

If you see an ad that seems inappropriate or unsuitable for the game's age rating, tap
Settings > Report an ad in the game (it opens an email to me in your mail app), or email me at
the address at the bottom. Tell me roughly when you saw it and what it showed, and I'll report it
to Google and block it if I can.

## Crash reports (only if you say yes)
Crash reports are off until you agree. After your first run, the game asks once: "Help fix
crashes?". If you say yes, then whenever the game crashes or hits an error it sends a report to
Sentry, a crash-reporting service, so I can find and fix the problem. A report contains:
- what went wrong and where in the game's code;
- what the game was doing just before (for example "battle 3 won"), and the current run's
  mode, seed and number of moves, so I can replay the run up to the problem; and
- the game and iOS versions, your iPhone model, and technical details such as free memory,
  battery level and language setting.
The game also sends Sentry a short note when each play session starts and ends (whether it
crashed), so I can see how often crashes happen. These notes carry a random ID for this copy of
the game, which Sentry stores only in scrambled (hashed) form, to count how many phones had a
crash.
None of this contains your name, email, contacts, location or saved games, and Sentry is set not
to store your IP address. Sentry stores crash reports in the European Union (Frankfurt,
Germany). Crash reports are deleted automatically after at most 90 days. The session notes are
used only for crash-rate figures and are kept as long as Sentry keeps those figures. None of this
is used for ads or shared with anyone else.

To change your answer at any time: in the game, Settings > Send crash reports (on or off).
Sentry's privacy policy: https://sentry.io/privacy/

## Leaderboards (Game Center)
If you're signed in to Game Center, the game sends your Long Night and Daily Dread scores to it.
Your Game Center nickname, picture and scores can be seen by other Game Center players. Game
Center is run by Apple under its own privacy policy:
https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/game-center/ . I can see the leaderboards like
anyone else, but Apple doesn't give me your Apple Account details. If you don't want this, sign
out of Game Center (iPhone Settings > Game Center); the game works the same without it.

## Sharing a trophy
If you share a trophy card, it goes only to the app you choose. If you pick Save Image, your
iPhone asks whether the game may add photos; it can only add the picture, not see your library.

## Your saved games
Your progress is saved in files on your iPhone, inside the game's own storage. I can't see them.
Like other app data, they may be included in your iPhone or iCloud backups, which Apple runs
under its own privacy policy and your own settings. Deleting the game deletes its saves.

## If you're testing a beta through TestFlight
Apple's TestFlight app shares some information about beta testers with developers, so we can
find and fix problems:
- your name and email address, if you were invited by email;
- when you installed and opened the beta, and your device model and iOS version;
- crash reports; and
- any feedback, comments and screenshots you choose to send.

I use this only to fix and improve the game. I don't sell it or share it with anyone else, and I
delete it when it's no longer needed for that, or sooner if you ask. Apple's handling of this
information is covered by Apple's own privacy policy and the TestFlight terms.

## If you email me
If you write to me, I'll receive your email address and whatever you send. I use it only to
answer you, and I'll delete it if you ask.

## The services I use
RevenueCat, Google and Sentry process information for the game as described above, under their
own privacy policies (linked above). I only use services that commit to protecting this
information at least as well as this page describes. Some of them process information in other
countries, including the United States.

## Deleting your information
- Saves and everything stored on your iPhone: delete the game.
- The random IDs used by RevenueCat and Sentry: deleting the game resets them; reinstalling
  starts new ones.
- Crash reports: deleted automatically within 90 days.
- Anything else: email me. Because I don't know which random ID is yours, I may not be able to
  find your records from your email alone, but I'll do what I can.

## Children
The game isn't made for children and isn't in the App Store's Kids category. It doesn't ask
anyone for personal information. If you think a child has sent me information (for example by
email), write to me and I'll delete it.

## Changes to this policy
If the game adds or changes how it uses information, I'll update this page before the version
of the game with that change is released, and the "Last updated" date above will change.
